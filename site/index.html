Origin story

HacKiroTube is a catalog of the YouTube videos Hacker News keeps telling people to watch, plus a graveyard for the ones that have since died, and an attempt to bring them back.

Where the idea came from

The idea is Andrej Karpathy's. He built HackerTube: an LLM read tens of thousands of HN comments and listed the videos the community recommends. I read about it in the news (TokenPost) and rebuilt my own version from scratch, for fun, for the Kiroween 2026 Halloween contest. All credit for the concept goes to him.

The name is Hacker News + Kiro + HackerTube.

What's new here

Click enough old HN links and you hit "Video unavailable". Twenty years of link rot took talks people still cite. So HacKiroTube also counts the dead:

  • Graveyard. 791 recommended videos are deleted or private. For each one the Necromancer finds a Wayback Machine snapshot from when HN first linked it, recovers the title, and searches for a re-upload. So far it has dug up … tombstones: … possibly resurrected, … archived, … lost. A ghost rises only where a re-upload was found; a cracked stone means it's gone.
  • Immortals. … videos recommended by 5 or more people across 10 or more years.
  • Séance. An MCP server, so you can ask Kiro for "a classic talk on distributed systems" and get the catalog back.

How it was made

  1. Harvester: 283,173 HN comments with YouTube links, 189,162 unique videos.
  2. Census: keeps videos at least 3 different people recommended, then asks YouTube which are still alive.
  3. Exorcist: the only LLM step. A small model sorts … living videos into crypts and writes why people recommend each one.
  4. Necromancer: archive.org and YouTube search, no LLM.

Cheap before smart: everything regex and API checks can remove goes before the LLM sees it. HackerTube used 504M input tokens; this used about 2.5M for twice the videos. Built in Kiro with specs, steering, a test hook and the Séance.

The rules of the house

No accounts, no backend, no hosted video. Links and embeds only; the dead point to the Internet Archive. Watched marks stay in your browser. The code is on GitHub under the MIT licence.
